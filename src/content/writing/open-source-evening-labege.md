Last night I went to "Open Source : de l'alternative à l'évidence", a meetup organised by OCCOS, the open source cluster of the Occitanie region. It took place at Kamin 231 in Labège, next to Toulouse.

Two talks stood out for me.

Thomas Riboulet, from WA Systems, told the story of 25 years of open source, from 2000 to 2026. Projects that people used to see as "the alternative" are now the foundations of the tech we use every day. They got there because of their quality and because they last.

Benoit Penicaut, from Nextcloud, talked about digital sovereignty and keeping control of your data. Nextcloud is a collaborative suite that is fully open source, and it shows that you can own your data without giving up features. As someone who is learning databases, I like this idea: knowing where your data lives starts with the database.

There were also talks by Stéphane Schwartz, from Sicoval, about digital sovereignty for the local area, and by Killian Larcher, from Soluce Technologies, who presented Umami. I liked Umami so much that I now use it on this site.

I didn't know there was such an active open source community so close to Toulouse. If you work on Postgres or open source in the area, I'm happy to talk.
