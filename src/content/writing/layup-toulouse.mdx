On October 25, I went to Layup, a street art exhibition in Toulouse. Kind of the opposite of the Musée des Augustins: no 14th century convent here, just a whole office building taken over by graffiti artists.

Layup is run by a collective of Toulouse artists, and this is their 6th edition. This year it's at 22 boulevard de la Marquette, in Compans-Caffarelli. Around 80 artists, every room is different: graffiti, calligraphy, hyperrealism, installations, photography. And it's free.

<Figure src={layupBuilding} alt="The Layup building, a red brick office block with the Layup logo above the entrance" caption="The building" />

I walked through the whole thing, and somehow I only thought to take photos of one room. So this post is about that room.

<Pair>
  <Figure src={layupCityWide} alt="A miniature graffiti city with skyscrapers, water towers and spray cans on a pink floor" caption="The whole room" />
  <Figure src={layupCityDetail} alt="Close-up of the buildings, a yellow crane and painted characters" caption="Up close" />

It's a full mini city. Cardboard skyscrapers, New York water towers, a little subway car hanging from the ceiling, a "Brooklyn / Flatbush" street sign, a "One Way" sign, and spray cans lying around everywhere like they're part of the streets. The ceiling is covered in paper flowers, the floor is pink, and the walls are full of graffiti and characters.

The closer you look, the more details you find. Every building is tagged, there's a tiny yellow crane, smiley faces on the rooftops, a truck covered in graffiti. I stayed in front of it way longer than I planned.

Next time I'll take more photos. If you're in Toulouse, it's on until mid-November, so go quickly.
